Both start from the same place. The record belongs to the person it is about, and it should live in their pocket. Three million people have already acted on that belief by choosing Evergreen. They are exactly the people who would want to keep their appointments too.

Before, during and after the appointment.

You lit up at the before and the after. Here is how the pieces line up.

  1. Before

    askmyGP, and earlier still, Neighbourhood Signals

    askmyGP carries the patient's own words to the practice. Neighbourhood Signals listens even earlier, in the space before demand, when someone first notices a rash or feels low and goes looking for help.

  2. During

    MyScribe

    One question on the patient's phone: do you mind if I record this? Just to help me remember. Then the conversation is kept, faithfully.

  3. After

    MyScribe and Afternote

    The appointment goes home with the person who was in it, into the record they already keep, in the clinician's own words, readable on a tired day and shareable with whoever they choose.

askmyGP holds the patient's words on the way in.

MyScribe holds the clinician's words on the way out.

The one thing your 3 million users can't keep yet.

Their Evergreen record already holds GP records, prescriptions, results and vaccinations. What it can't hold is what was actually said in the room: the part people forget first and need most. That is the entry MyScribe adds.
